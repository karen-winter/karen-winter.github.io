Cosmetic Ingredient Analysis

I collected the ingredient lists for 392 beauty and personal care products by hand. I wanted to see if what's in a product lines up with its EWG score.

What I found

Fragrance made the biggest difference. Products with fragrance averaged an EWG score of 5.56. Products without it averaged 3.15. This held in every category that had fragranced products.
Ingredients I avoid push the score up. 141 of my products (36%) have at least one ingredient I avoid. They average 4.50, compared to 2.96 for products without them.
More than a quarter of my products have a high-risk ingredient. 110 products (28%) contain at least one ingredient with an EWG score of 8 or higher. I was surprised by that.
Longer ingredient lists tend to score higher. Products with 1 to 10 ingredients averaged 1.94. Products with 31 or more averaged 4.37.

These show what goes together in my data. They don't prove that one thing causes another.

How I built it

1. Collecting and cleaning the data in Google Sheets

I started this sheet just for myself, to keep track of what's in the products I use. When I turned it into a project, I had to clean it up and split it into proper tables:

  • product: one row per product, with its brand, category and EWG score
  • ingredient: one row per ingredient, with its EWG score range and my flags (avoid, carcinogen, acne and more)
  • product_ingredient: which ingredients are in which product
  • ing_map: matches 880 different label spellings to one standard ingredient name

I standardized brand names with TRIM, PROPER, CLEAN and REGEXREPLACE, made sure the EWG columns only held numbers, removed duplicate products, and cleaned up product names. I also set my own low, medium and high risk tiers.

2. Analyzing it in MySQL

I loaded the 4 tables into MySQL and wrote 12 queries. They use joins, CASE, GROUP BY WITH ROLLUP and HAVING to compare EWG scores by category, brand, fragrance and the ingredients I avoid. Each query has a comment with what I found.

Limits of this data

  • Most of these are products I use, so it's not a random sample of what's on the market.
  • EWG scores are one organization's ratings. Other sources may rate the same ingredient differently.
  • The risk tiers and the avoid list are my own choices.

Product and ingredient scores come from EWG's Skin Deep database (ewg.org/skindeep). EWG has not reviewed or endorsed this analysis.
